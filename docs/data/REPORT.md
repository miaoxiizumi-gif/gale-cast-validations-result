## Limitations

- **L-01** The evaluation period was used historically for selection; all scores are retrospective and exploratory.
- **L-02** Only 27 events, all pressure falls; event metrics have wide intervals, and no gale-force wind event exists to evaluate.
- **L-03** W1's event scores depend strongly on input scaling; which scaling the historical decisions assumed differs from training.
- **L-04** CSV files are assumed, not proven, to equal the database tables W1 was trained and scored on.
- **L-05** Next-day only; no valid two- or three-day procedure exists for W1.
- **L-06** The block bootstrap (14-day blocks) captures short-range dependence only; seasonal dependence is not fully represented.
- **L-07** Supplementary data are owner-confirmed PAGASA observations without an independent source document; their contribution to W1 cannot be separated.
- **L-08** The running Hugging Face container was not inspected; deployment identity rests on the Space repository contents.
- **L-09** Humidity, rainfall, pressure and direction outputs were not re-evaluated against baselines.

## Paper-ready paragraphs

### P-1 Methods: evaluation design

The frozen deployed model (W1; SHA-256 f2db5aa9…9d14b0) was evaluated retrospectively without retraining or recalibration. For each of the three primary stations and each target date from 15 October 2021 to 31 December 2024, a next-day forecast was issued from the 32 preceding daily records, scaled with the station-specific scalers used in training. Forecasts were compared with two reference forecasts on identical cases: persistence (the latest observation) and climatology (station-by-month means from 2015 to 14 October 2020). Wind speed was verified against unimputed observations using MAE, RMSE and bias; events under the study label (wind ≥ 10.8 m/s or a 24-hour pressure fall ≥ 3 hPa) were verified with contingency-table measures and PR-AUC. Uncertainty was estimated with a paired circular block bootstrap over dates (14-day blocks, 2,000 resamples). The evaluation specification was fixed before scoring.

### P-2 Results: wind

On 3,520 identical cases, W1's next-day wind forecasts had an MAE of 1.021 m/s, compared with 0.467 m/s for persistence and 0.584 m/s for climatology; both differences were statistically established. The model's forecasts varied little (standard deviation 0.27 m/s against 1.16 m/s observed). Lower MAE indicates a smaller average numerical error on the same cases; on this measure W1 provides no improvement over simple references, and the result says nothing about gale-force winds, none of which occurred.

### P-3 Results: events

The evaluation period contained 27 hazard-label events, all defined by pressure falls. With cross-station averaging and the adopted threshold of 0.601, W1 detected 16 events with 8 false alarms (precision 0.667, recall 0.593, F1 0.627), outperforming event persistence (F1 0.226); its PR-AUC was 0.487 against a prevalence of 0.0077. Its scores were not calibrated (Brier skill relative to climatology -2.80). When inputs were scaled as in the historical scorecard, the same procedure reproduced the previously reported PR-AUC of 0.830, showing that the reported performance depends strongly on input preprocessing.

### P-4 Limitations

The evaluation period was used historically to compare model variants and to choose the scoring procedure, and training included records from other stations dated within it; results are therefore retrospective rather than independent. The event sample is small and consists entirely of pressure falls, so no conclusion about gale-force wind detection is possible. No training history or random seed was preserved, so training cannot be reproduced. Only next-day forecasts were assessed. A prospective evaluation on observations from 2025 onward is required before any operational claim.
